## One village, two ways

Golden Sun opens on a stormy night in Vale, when Isaac and Garet are still
children. Most of the village that night isn't the Vale you'll walk around
later. It's a stand-in, [vale_storm.c](/tbs/source/src/scene/vale_storm-c),
with a map of its own, and the doors that lead into the village by day lead
into it instead. When it starts, it sets a story flag, `FLAG_VALE_STORM`, that
tells the rest of Vale the storm is on.

The rest of Vale doesn't get a copy. The part with the town gate,
[vale_home.c](/tbs/source/src/scene/vale_home-c), is one scene for every point
in the story: the storm, the morning three years later, the farewell and every
visit after that. Each time you arrive, it checks the story flags and picks
who stands where and what they say. So the game does both: when a place
changes completely, it gets a scene of its own, and when only its people
change, the scene works it out from the flags.

The night ends at that gate, too. Two strangers fight the boys, and whether
you win or lose, the battle returns to the same spot, the storm flag is
cleared, and the scene's exit leads to the title screen at the top of this
page. When it fades out, it's three years later.

## Leaving home

Three years later, the Wise One sends Isaac and Garet after the Elemental
Stars stolen from Sol Sanctum, and the whole village gathers at the gate to
see them off.

![The people of Vale call out their farewell as Isaac and Garet leave through the town gate](/story/vale-farewell.gif)

Until that moment, the gate does nothing. Stepping on it only leads out once
`FLAG_PARTY_LEFT_VALE` is set, so the first time you leave Vale, it's the
farewell that walks you out, by the same exit the gate uses from then on.

## The whole world

Outside Vale, the world map is drawn tilted in perspective around the party,
on two background layers the hardware can scale and rotate: the ground, and
the mountains, woods and towns over it. Only the squares around the party are
in video memory at a time: `Map_UpdateCurrentTileBlock` loads the block
around you into both layers. This picture moves that point across the whole
map, asks the game for every block in turn and lays them out flat, one pixel
for each pixel of the map.

<Picture src="/story/world.png" alt="The whole world map of The Broken Seal, laid out flat" style={{ width: '100%', imageRendering: 'pixelated' }} />

Laid out like this, the map shows a few things the game never lets you see at
once. Crossbone Isle is there, though in The Lost Age the map you can open says
of it, “This place doesn't seem to appear on the map...”. Idejima is there
twice, joined to the coast and adrift. And the flat green and grey blocks in
the west and south, and the white strip beside the snow in the north, are
squares the map's data leaves blank.

## The beacon

The game ends at the top of Venus Lighthouse. The party beats Saturos and
Menardi there, but Saturos throws an Elemental Star into the lighthouse
anyway, and the game cuts to the world map as its beacon lights up.

![Venus Lighthouse's beacon flares up on the world map](/story/venus-lighthouse-lit.gif)

That cut belongs to the world map scene. When the aerie sends the party to the
world map's entrance 91, [world_map.c](/tbs/source/src/scene/world_map-c)
plays the lighthouse sequence instead of letting you walk, then sends you
straight back up to the aerie, at its entrance 93, for the last battle.
Mercury Lighthouse uses the same code from entrance 90.
